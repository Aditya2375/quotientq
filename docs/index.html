Source, CLI & exact conventions ↗

A finite Markov decision problem

State is steps remaining (1..12), inventory (−2..2) and observed signal (−1/+1). The signal predicts an up move with probability 0.35 or 0.65 and persists with probability 0.7. Time is in the state; step 12 is true termination, not an arbitrary training cutoff. Mid starts at 100; each step moves ±1.

Quotes before future draws

Tight quotes sit 0.5 from mid; wide quotes 1.5. Skew quotes 1.5 on the inventory-increasing side and 0.5 on the reducing side (1/1 when flat). Side fill probability is 0.4/(1+distance) times an adverse/benign multiplier: 1.5/0.5 in training, 2.5/0.2 in stress. Buys are adverse on down moves, sells on up moves. The environment couples fills to the later move; the policy cannot see that move or its uniforms.

Cash is not the objective

Buy/sell fills happen at opening quotes, then mid moves. One lot per side, both cap checks against prior inventory. Fee is 0.03 per fill. Reward = marked-wealth change −0.08 × after-fill inventory², less 0.25 per terminal unit for forced exit. Unlimited funding/borrow and exit liquidity are assumed. No queue, latency, partial fills or exchange mechanics.

Sample, freeze, evaluate

Zero-initialized Q updates use α = 1 / (1 + 0.05 N), ε = 0.2 and undiscounted max-next-Q targets. Terminal continuation is zero. Test and oracle never enter training. The greedy policy uses the first action within 10⁻¹² of the maximum, for numerical ties. Fixed seeds and finite budgets prove no convergence, robustness or real profitability.
